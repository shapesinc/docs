Think of the context window as the AI's "working memory"—a dedicated space where all relevant information is stored and processed for each reply.

## What's Included in the Context Window?

shapes.inc combines several key inputs into its context window:

**User Input in Personality Fields** – Your Shape's name, backstory, and personality settings are included to maintain consistent tone and character throughout the conversation.

**Response Style** – The Shape's **Response style** (in the Shape editor under **Personality**) establishes its fundamental style and behavioral patterns. Options include **Human**, **Short creative**, and **Long creative**, or you can write your own **Instructions**, which switches the style to **Custom**.

**Short-Term Memory** – The most recent messages from your chat history. Shape owners control how many recent messages are included under **Memory** → **Short-term memory** in the Shape editor. Premium plans allow more recent messages per Shape.

**Long-Term Memory** – When enabled, your Shape can access and reference facts, events, and details from previous conversations, plus notes people asked it to remember. Shape owners manage it under **Memory** → **Long-term memory** in the Shape editor. See the [Memory Guide](/memory).

**Knowledge & Training** – Custom training, lore, facts, and other knowledge entries you've provided to guide your Shape's responses and expertise.

**Your Profile and Preset** – The details in your Profile (such as **About you**) and your personal preset from **Settings** → **Personalization** are sent with your messages. Long Profile details take up space, so keep them focused.

**Chat Instructions** – Anything in the chat's **Instructions** (in the chat's **Settings** → **AI Configurations**) is also sent to the Shape and included in the context window for each message.

All of this information is packaged together and sent to the AI engine with every message you send.

## How AI Engines Affect Context Windows

Different AI engines have varying **context window sizes**, measured in tokens (small chunks of text). A larger context window allows the AI to consider more information simultaneously—including more chat history, personality details, and knowledge entries. Conversely, a smaller context window means the AI may lose track of older conversation elements more quickly. Some premium AI Engines offer larger context windows; check the [live engine catalog](https://shapes.inc/engines) for current details.

## What Happens When the Context Window Fills Up?

When the context window reaches capacity, the system must make space for new information by dropping older content. This can result in:

- The AI forgetting earlier parts of the conversation
- Loss of important details like names, events, or user preferences
- Responses that feel disconnected from the broader conversation context

## The Performance Impact of Information Overload

While it might seem beneficial to include as much information as possible, overwhelming the context window can actually hurt performance. Excessive amounts of knowledge entries, overly detailed instructions, or extremely long conversations can cause the AI to:

- Become confused and mix up details
- Generate shorter or lower-quality responses
- Hallucinate facts or ignore parts of its instructions

## Best Practices for Optimal Performance

To get the best results from your Shape:

**Keep it focused** – Write concise, targeted custom instructions and personality fields rather than lengthy descriptions.

**Be selective with knowledge** – Only include large amounts of knowledge when truly necessary for your Shape's purpose.

**Manage memory thoughtfully** – Consider whether you need every past message to remain in context, or if recent conversations are sufficient.

**Upgrade if necessary** – Some premium AI Engines have larger context windows, and [Premium](/premium-subscriptions) plans let each Shape read more recent messages. If your use case requires a large amount of input, a premium engine may give better results.

## Key Takeaway

The context window serves as your Shape's working memory. Keeping it clear, focused, and well-organized helps ensure your Shape responds accurately, consistently, and with the right level of detail for meaningful conversations.
