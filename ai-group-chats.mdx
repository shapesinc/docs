A multiplayer assistant helps a group move from different preferences to a decision and the work that follows. On shapes.inc, that happens in a shared chat with the people involved and AI assistants called **Shapes**.

Roommates can compare couches against everyone's budget and space. A family can work out travel dates. Classmates can turn a project brief into a plan. Everyone can contribute context, question the assistant's suggestions, and see the same result.

## What Makes AI Group Chats Different

The assistant works with the group's conversation. One person might care about price, another about accessibility, and another about timing. Put those constraints in the chat so the Shape can help compare the options and identify what still needs an answer.

A useful Shape contributes its own research, drafts, and suggestions. The people in the chat can correct it and decide what to use. Its role is to help the whole group make progress.

A chat can be a **Chat**, for talking things through, or an **Agent** chat, where Shapes can search the web, run code, and use apps people have connected. **Auto**, the default for new chats, chooses for each message. In group Agent chats, Shapes reply when they can help and stay out of ordinary conversation. See [Chat and Agent](/chat-and-agent-modes).

## A Good Multiplayer Assistant

We build toward three principles:

- **Earn the group's trust.** Learn what the group values, how it makes judgments, and which risks it is willing to take. Make assumptions visible so people can correct them.
- **Participate as an equal actor.** Contribute a perspective that everyone can challenge, and consider every participant's needs. Creating or inviting a Shape does not make one person's preferences the group's decision.
- **Proactively add value.** Bring useful information, identify an unresolved tradeoff, or draft the next step when it helps. Being useful also means knowing when to leave space for people to talk.

You can guide this behavior through [chat instructions](/custominstructions-chats), [memory](/memory), and [AI replies settings](/designing-social-intelligence). These controls help shape the assistant's behavior; they do not guarantee agreement or a correct answer.

## What People Use AI Group Chats For

- **Shared purchases:** list each person's priorities, compare the options the group has found, and explain the tradeoffs.
- **Travel and family plans:** collect dates, budgets, and accessibility needs, then draft an itinerary for everyone to review.
- **Study and projects:** turn a brief into tasks, explain difficult concepts, and surface unanswered questions.
- **Team decisions:** summarize a discussion, compare proposals, and draft next steps with owners and open questions.

For fresh research or work in an outside app, use an Agent chat and check the available [Shape Skills](/shapeskills). A Shape can use an app like Gmail or Notion only after someone connects it under [Connected apps](/connected-apps); adding a Shape does not connect everyone's accounts. Shapes can also set up [live polls and forms](/multiplayer-activities) so everyone can weigh in.

## How to Start a Group Chat With AI

1. Select **New Chat** in the sidebar. On a phone, tap **New**.
2. Select a Shape under **Shapes**. Switch to **People** to add the people involved.
3. Select **Continue**, choose a chat name, and select **Create Chat**.
4. Send a message describing the decision or task you want help with.
5. To invite more people later, select **Invite** at the top of the chat and use **Copy Link** or **Share via...**. To add people or Shapes directly, select the chat's name to open its **Settings**, then choose **Participants** → **Add participants**.

Ask each person to add their priorities before the Shape recommends an answer. For example:

> We're choosing a couch for our apartment. Our total budget is $900. One of us needs washable fabric, and one needs enough space to stretch out. Help us compare the options we share, explain the compromises, and ask about anything we have missed.

Select the chat's name at the top to open its **Settings**. From there you can manage notifications, participants, and shared files; chat owners also find AI settings, chat appearance, and moderation. See the [new chat walkthrough](/faq/how-do-i-start-a-new-chat) for each step and [Chats 101](/chats-101) for the detailed controls.

## Give the Chat Shared Context

Name the chat for its purpose, such as “Apartment decisions” or “Science project.” Put standing requirements in [Custom Chat Instructions](/custominstructions-chats), and pin the brief or current decision so people can find it.

Keep the assistant informed when plans change. Use [memory controls](/memory) to review or correct remembered information. If a side discussion needs its own space, use [Branch Off](/branch-off). Use [Groups](/groups) to organize related chats.

## When the Chat Needs Adjustment

- **A Shape is quiet:** mention it with `@`, check the selected engine and any credit requirement, and review [reply settings](/shapes101#activating-shapes).
- **Too many responses:** review [AI replies](/designing-social-intelligence) and turn off unwanted automatic triggers.
- **It misses a requirement:** restate the requirement, update [chat instructions](/custominstructions-chats), and check [memory](/memory).
- **Someone cannot join:** send a fresh link from **Invite** and review the [chat lock settings](/faq/how-do-i-lock-a-chat-so-that-other-people-cant-join).
- **Credits are being used unexpectedly:** open **Generation details** from the reply's ⋯ menu to see the engine and credits used, then check [credit preferences](/faq/why-is-a-free-engine-costing-credits).
