A shape does better work from a short brief than from a long prompt. Say the outcome you want, the context it can't see, the limits, what done looks like, and where to stop.

Web search, purchases, phone calls, reminders, and connected apps run in Agent chats. **Auto**, the default for new chats, chooses Agent when a request needs it. See [Chat, Agent, and Auto](/chat-and-agent-modes).

## The five parts of a good request

| Part | What to say | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Outcome | The result you want, not the steps | "Three dinner options for Friday" |
| Context | What the shape can't see: who, when, where, what's decided | "6 of us, one vegan, near the station" |
| Limits | Budget, sources, what to leave alone | "Under 40 each" |
| Done | What a finished answer contains | "Links, then a poll" |
| Stop point | Where it hands back to you | "Don't book anything" |

A quick question needs only the outcome. A request that spends money or contacts someone needs all five.

## Build a request up

A weak request leaves the shape to guess the dates, the budget, and how far to go:

```text wrap
Find us a place to stay in Lisbon.
```

The same request with all five parts:

```text wrap
Find a place to stay in Lisbon for four of us, May 8 to 15. Budget is 1,200 total for the week, and Sam needs step-free access. Post the best three with the total price, cancellation terms, and links, then make a poll. Don't book anything. Maya pays once we've voted.
```

The shape searches, posts three options and a poll, and stops. Booking starts when Maya asks for the chosen option, and the shape pauses again for her to review the total before she pays.

## Ask in a group

In a group Agent chat, shapes stay out of ordinary conversation. Mention the shape by name, then say what you need.

- **Name who decides.** "Maya picks" or "we vote" tells the shape whether to recommend one option or set up a choice.
- **Ask for options in the chat.** A comparison with links, or a [poll](/multiplayer-activities#vote-in-a-poll-card), lets everyone check the same result.
- **Say whose constraints count.** Each person states their own dates, budget, and needs in the chat.
- **Name who pays.** The person who asks for a purchase or booking is the payer and approves it. Other members can't approve for them.

The same goes for [phone calls](/phone-calls#in-a-group-chat): the person who asks answers the call's questions and pays for it. A shape uses your connected apps for your own requests, and for a teammate's only as [**Shared access**](/connected-apps#let-teammates-use-your-apps) allows.

## Say where to stop

A clear request is the go-ahead for most work, so put the stop point in it.

A shape does this without asking:

- Searches, reads, compares, and drafts.
- Sends a message you asked for directly. "Email Sam that I'm running late" is sent.
- Places a call when the request is clear. On the call it agrees only to the times, prices, or options you named.

A shape asks first before it pays, books, or deletes. Nothing is ordered until you select **Place order** or approve the payment on the purchase card. See [Websites and purchases](/websites-and-purchases#buy-something).

To see the words first, ask for a draft. To review every email, tell a shape "never send an email without asking me." The rule is saved. See [Ask before sending](/connected-apps#ask-before-sending).

When a shape asks you in the chat, restate the action instead of replying "go":

```text wrap
Yes, book the 7:30 table for six under my name.
```

A new request sent while an order is waiting counts as a new request, not as an answer to that order.

## Give it what it can't see

Point the shape at the source instead of retyping it.

- **Files.** Attach the brief, the quote, or the photo. See [Files and attachments](/files-and-attachments).
- **Links.** Share a public Google Docs, Sheets, Slides, or Drive link and the shape reads it.
- **Connected apps.** Connect your calendar, inbox, or docs and the shape checks them for your requests. See [Connected apps](/connected-apps).
- **Chat instructions.** A chat owner puts the group's goal, budget, and deadline in **Instructions** once. See [Chat instructions](/custominstructions-chats).
- **Memory.** Say "remember that Sam is vegetarian", or type `/remember` followed by the note. See [Memory](/memory).
- **Earlier conversation.** In Agent chats a shape searches the chat's history, so "the place Sam suggested" is enough. For another chat you're in, name it: "check what the trip chat said about dates."

## Requests that run later

A request with a time becomes a reminder, a routine, or a watch. Say what to do, when, and when to stop.

```text wrap
Check hourly for a reply to my venue enquiry. Tell this chat when the venue answers, summarize the dates and the quoted price, then stop watching.
```

Check the shape's reply to confirm it was scheduled. Cancel it, or pause a routine, under **Settings** → **Tasks**. See [Tasks and reminders](/tasks).

## Fix a reply that missed

- **Say what was wrong and what to keep.** "Keep the first two options. Replace the third with one under 150 a night."
- **Regenerate.** Open the ⋯ menu on the reply and select **Regenerate**. Credits come from whoever regenerates. See [Regenerate a reply](/regeneration).
- **Move focused work into a thread.** Ask for a thread, or type `/thread` followed by your message. See [Threads](/threads).
- **Branch off.** In a group chat, open the ⋯ menu on a message and choose **Branch off** to continue in a new private chat. See [Branch off](/branch-off).
- **Start a new chat.** When the conversation has drifted, type `/new` and state the request in full.

## Habits to drop

Older prompt tricks get in the way. Write the way you'd brief a capable friend.

- **Shouting in capitals.** Say it once in ordinary words, with the reason: "no shellfish, Drew is allergic."
- **"You are a world-class expert" openers.** Say who the answer is for: "explain it for someone who has never rented a car abroad."
- **Threats, pleading, or offers of a tip.** State the goal and the limit.
- **Repeating the same rule.** Say it once, or save it in **Instructions** or as a note.
- **Step-by-step scripts for simple goals.** Describe the result and let the shape choose the steps.
- **Pasting everything.** Share the one file or link that matters and say which part to use.
- **"Never" for things you sometimes want.** Write "ask me before you book", not "never book".

## Requests to copy

Change the details and send.

**Planning**

```text wrap
Find three dinner options near the station for Friday for 6 people, one vegan, under 40 each. Compare them with links, then make a poll so we can choose. Don't book anything.
```

```text wrap
Check my calendar for Saturday and suggest two times for the study group. Don't add an event until we pick one.
```

```text wrap
Work through these three apartments with us in a thread. Use the budget and move-in date we've shared, pick one and say why, and post only the final recommendation in the main chat.
```

**Buying and booking**

```text wrap
Compare three standing desks under 400 that deliver by the 20th, with links. I'm paying. Wait for me to choose one before you start checkout.
```

```text wrap
Reorder the coffee I bought last month. My budget is 30 all-in, so tell me if the total is higher before I approve.
```

```text wrap
Search flights from Boston to Lisbon for four people, leaving May 8 and back May 15. Post the best three with the fare type and cancellation rules. Don't book anything.
```

**Calls**

```text wrap
Call Lucia's and ask for a table for 6 on Friday at 7:30. Anything between 7 and 8:30 is fine. Book it under my name. If they need anything else from me, ask me during the call.
```

```text wrap
Call the hardware store on Main Street and ask if they have the 12-foot extension ladder in stock today. Only ask about stock and price. Don't order one.
```

**Team work**

```text wrap
Draft a reply to the venue from my email and show it here first. Don't send it until I approve the wording.
```

```text wrap
Every Monday at 9 a.m. Eastern, post what's still open on our move-out checklist. Open items only, five lines at most.
```

```text wrap
Start a new chat with Maya and Drew to plan Saturday's picnic. Carry over our agreed date, headcount, and food budget, and nothing else.
```
