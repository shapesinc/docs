If your team already talks in Slack, you can bring it with you: import your history, keep channels in sync while people switch over, and send updates from the tools you use into the right chat, where shapes can read them and help.

All of this lives in a [workspace](/sidebar-organization). Open **Settings → Slack** to start. In your personal space, you'll be asked to create a workspace first. In a workspace you don't own, ask a workspace owner to connect Slack.

## Import your Slack history

1. In Slack, export your workspace. Slack workspace admins can do this from Slack's export settings; you'll get a .zip file.
2. In shapes.inc, open **Settings → Slack → Bring your history** and drop the .zip in. It's read in your browser, not uploaded as a file.
3. Choose a workspace you own, or create a new one.
4. Pick what to bring. Archived channels and DMs are off by default, and you'll only be offered DMs you were part of.
5. Leave **Invite by email** on to invite the people from your Slack workspace, or turn it off to invite them later.

Each channel becomes a chat with its history, threads, and reactions. Messages keep their authors' Slack names and pictures with a **Slack** badge, and files come through as links to Slack. Canvases, huddles, custom emoji images, edit history, pins, and bookmarks aren't imported.

Imported chats start with only you in them, until the people you invited join. You can run the import again later; messages already imported aren't duplicated.

## Keep Slack in sync

Workspace owners can connect Slack so new messages flow both ways while people move over:

1. Select **Connect Slack** in **Settings → Slack** or the workspace menu, then **Allow** in Slack.
2. On **Add your Slack channels**, pick channels. Public channels are selected for you. Select **Add** to finish.

After an import, the last step also offers **Keep in sync with Slack**.

- New messages in either place show up in the other.
- New public channels get a chat automatically. For a private channel, type `/invite @shapes.inc` in that channel in Slack.
- Channel renames carry over.
- People in a channel whose email matches a shapes.inc account are added to its chat. People from other organizations in shared channels aren't added.

In a chat's **Settings → Integrations → Slack**, owners can set which direction messages sync, turn on **Shapes reply to @mentions from Slack**, or **Unlink** the channel.

Not synced yet: edits, deletions, and reactions made in shapes.inc; file contents; DMs; archived channels; and people leaving a channel.

## Post updates with hooks

A hook gives a chat its own web address that a tool can post to: a new pull request, a Linear issue, a card transaction, a build that failed. Updates show up as cards with an **APP** badge, and shapes in the chat can read them, so you can ask "anything from GitHub today I should look at?"

1. Open the chat's **Settings → Integrations**. Only chat owners can, and not in DMs.
2. Select **New hook**, choose **GitHub**, **Linear**, **Ramp**, **Slack-compatible**, or **Generic JSON**, name it, and select **Create**.
3. Copy the address with **Copy URL** and paste it into the tool's webhook settings. The address is shown once. For GitHub, leave the secret blank.

Treat the address like a password: anyone who has it can post into the chat. Use **Rotate URL** to replace it, or **Delete** to remove the hook. Messages it already posted stay.

**Let shapes respond** is off by default. Turn it on and up to 5 shapes in the chat can reply to each update, charged to the person who created the hook like any other message.

| Limit | |
|---|---|
| Hooks per chat | 20 |
| Updates per hook | 120 a minute |
| Update size | 1MB; long text is shortened |
