# Documentation terminology

- The platform, product, and company name is **Shapes, Inc.** Always include the comma and period.
- Never refer to the platform as **Shapes**. For example, write **On Shapes, Inc.** instead of **On Shapes**.
- A **Shape** is an AI assistant that participates in a shared room on Shapes, Inc. Use **Shapes** only as the plural of those AI participants.
- Preserve exact interface labels when documenting navigation. Labels such as **Your Shapes** and **New Chat** are correct even when the surrounding explanation uses assistant and room terminology.
- Before finishing documentation changes, check that every use of **Shapes** clearly refers to AI participants; otherwise, change it to **Shapes, Inc.**

# Product positioning

- Lead with **multiplayer assistant**: people researching, weighing preferences, making decisions, and getting things done together.
- Explain features through their contribution to a shared goal. Use examples from friends, couples, roommates, families, classmates, and teams.
- The guiding principles are earning the group's trust, participating as an equal actor with its own perspective, and proactively adding useful work. Do not equate proactivity with more messages.
- Personality and creative or social uses can support collaboration; entertainment, companionship, and “talk to AI with friends” are not the primary product promise.
- Keep feature steps and troubleshooting concrete. Verify interface labels and capabilities; distinguish product direction from available behavior. Do not promise automatic app access, perfect memory, unlimited usage, or actions that depend on unconfigured skills.
