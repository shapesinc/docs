A trip, an exam, or something you want to build gives you a reason to spend time together. Start a chat with the people involved, bring in what each person knows, and ask a shape to help you turn the conversation into something you can use.

Choose **New chat** and invite your people. Under **Shapes**, select **Auto** or choose an existing shape if you want an assistant. Creating a custom shape is optional. For research, files, connected apps, and coding, use [Agent mode](/chat-and-agent-modes). The workflows below combine features; their linked guides explain setup, access, and limits.

## Plan a trip with friends

Start with everyone's constraints. One person supplies possible dates, another the budget, and others add travel time, accessibility needs, and the things they hope to do. Use an [availability poll](/multiplayer-activities#vote-in-a-poll-card) if several dates could work. Keep private booking details for the booking forms later.

> Help us plan a weekend away. Maya can leave Friday evening, Drew needs to leave Saturday morning, and we want time outdoors without a long hike. Summarize the dates and budget we've shared, ask for missing constraints, and research two realistic plans with sources.

The shape can use [web research](/shapeskills#research-options) to compare travel, accommodation, and activities. Ask for the source links and total assumptions, then have people check the parts that matter to them. A [thread](/threads) can keep accommodation research together while other planning continues in the main chat; bring changed dates or budgets into that thread when they matter.

Once you have real options, ask for a [choice card](/multiplayer-activities#choose-between-options) so people can weigh in and the requester can settle the choice, or a [shortlist page](/multiplayer-activities#compare-a-shortlist-together) for a detailed comparison. The shape can summarize the result. A vote helps you decide; it does not reserve anything. Resolve any remaining objections, then ask for a day-by-day itinerary and a downloadable plan with the agreed dates, meeting points, costs, and open questions. Open the [file](/files-and-attachments) together and correct it before relying on it.

When you are ready to book, the person paying asks for the chosen itinerary for the named travellers. Where [travel booking](/websites-and-purchases#book-travel) is available, each traveller in the chat enters their own details privately; the payer supplies the booking contact and guest details, reviews the final quote, and completes the offered payment flow. Everyone can follow the shared trip status. Check each booking confirmation, because one confirmed item does not mean the whole trip succeeded.

After the bookings are settled, one person can ask the shape to add the agreed event to their [connected calendar](/connected-apps#find-a-time-for-a-study-group), naming the time and invitees. Check the event in the calendar. Ask separately for a [reminder in the chat](/tasks) to confirm travel arrangements or finish a [shared packing checklist](/multiplayer-activities#keep-a-checklist-or-collect-responses). Check that the reminder appears in **Settings → Tasks**; an itinerary file does not schedule it automatically.

## Study together

Put the material and the questions in the same chat. One person can [attach](/files-and-attachments) the assignment, another their notes, and another a worked example. Add a message identifying each file and the parts you need help with. A successful upload does not mean the shape could read every page or format, so ask which material it used.

> We are revising these chapters together. Explain the part our answers disagree on, using the attached course notes. Give us a worked example, then make a quiz so we can decide what to practise next.

Ask the shape to explain one concept at a time, and let each person challenge the reasoning or try the next step. Use a thread for a difficult question so the explanation and follow-ups stay together. Check claims and calculations against the course material; ask the shape to identify gaps instead of guessing what an unreadable file contains.

A [quiz card](/multiplayer-activities#study-or-play-a-quiz-together) lets everyone answer in the chat. Each person sees the correct answer after answering, and the group can review scores and choose the next topic. Answers and scores belong to the shared activity. Ask for a fresh explanation of a missed question rather than treating a score as proof that everyone understands it.

Finish by asking for a revision file with the corrected explanations, useful examples, and remaining practice questions. Someone should open the actual download and check that the group's corrections are included. Ask the shape to [remind the chat](/tasks) at a specific time and time zone before your next study session, then check the saved reminder. Next time, attach the revised material or point to the relevant discussion and ask for a recap; [memory](/memory) helps with continuity but is not a complete copy of every file and conversation.

## Build an app or a website together

Agree on one small result first: a page for a neighborhood event, a class project, or a fix to something your friends already use. One person explains the audience, another supplies examples or content, and another describes what should happen when someone uses it.

> Help us make a page for our neighborhood cleanup. Maya will check the wording and meeting point; Drew will try it on a phone. Start with a draft we can review here, then explain what is needed to publish it at our own site.

Choose where the work should happen before requesting changes:

- **A page in the chat:** ask for a self-contained page, calculator, or prototype the group can open and try. Use a [shared activity](/multiplayer-activities) when people need saved votes, answers, or checklist progress; a page with local controls does not automatically synchronize everyone's state.
- **Hosted coding:** ask for [Claude Code or Codex](/faq/how-do-free-coding-agent-limits-work) when the work needs a coding agent run by shapes.inc. These runs have their own allowances and do not use your personal computer or its local sign-in.
- **A Code Project:** connect an existing GitHub repository and environment under [Code Projects](/connect-your-computer#code-projects). It starts private. The owner can explicitly share it so current and future chat members can use that project, including its saved environment; this is separate from sharing a personal GitHub connection.
- **Your own computer:** [connect the machine](/connect-your-computer) that has the files and tools. Its owner can request work from the chat, including from a phone while the computer stays online. Other people need an explicit saved computer share to request work on it themselves; read-only access and permission to make changes are different.
- **An outside agent:** [invite an external agent](/ai-agents) that you or a teammate runs. It is a separate participant with its own host and tools. Joining the chat does not start or keep its responder running.

Use a [thread](/threads) for the build or review, and ask for the changed files, the preview or output, checks that actually ran, and anything unfinished. Maya can review content while Drew tries the controls and layout. Bring their findings back into the same thread and ask for the specific correction. Tool access and checks vary by environment; a hosted coding run does not guarantee that every visual or media check is available.

Distinguish the result you want: a draft page in a reply, files changed on a computer, a pushed Git branch, a pull request, and a deployed website are separate outcomes. Ask explicitly for the supported next step and check the actual file, branch, pull-request, or published URL. A finished coding status alone is not proof that the site is published or that the requested behavior works. See [coding limits](/faq/how-do-free-coding-agent-limits-work) and [credits](/credits) before a longer run.

## Make documents, images, and slides together

For a class presentation or a community event, gather the audience, facts, source files, and design preferences first. Ask for an outline or first draft in the chat so everyone can correct the substance before producing the final version.

One person can check the facts, another edit the wording, and another review the visuals. Ask for a [generated image](/shapeskills#create-and-discuss-images), a document, or a slide outline with speaker notes as the work requires. For slides, review the sequence and each slide's main point together; for a poster, check the generated text, date, and location.

Then ask for a [downloadable file](/files-and-attachments#ask-a-shape-to-make-a-file) in the format you need. Confirm the available tool can produce that format, open the returned file in the intended app, and check the actual pages or slides. A chat preview and a downloadable presentation are different results. Request corrections in the same chat and download the revised output before sharing it elsewhere.

Files can have shareable links outside the chat; follow the [file access guidance](/files-and-attachments#open-find-and-download-files). Creating a file does not email it, publish it, or update an original document in another app. If that is the next step, name the destination and request the action through a supported connection, then check the result there.

## What makes the group version better

Each person supplies information the others may not have. The shape can organize that input into a comparison, explanation, or draft, while everyone checks the same result and corrects it in place in the conversation.

Keep a visible recap of what you agreed, what is still uncertain, and who is taking the next step. When someone joins later, ask for a recap of the relevant discussion and let the group correct it. A shared recommendation is useful because people can inspect it; agreement still comes from the people involved.

## Set it up

1. Choose [New chat](/create-a-chat) and select the people to invite. Under **Shapes**, select **Auto** or choose an existing shape if you want an assistant. A custom shape is optional.
2. Use [Agent mode](/chat-and-agent-modes) for research and actions. Add the shared goal and lasting constraints to [Custom Chat Instructions](/custominstructions-chats).
3. Send the relevant files and links. Connect only the [apps](/connected-apps), [computer](/connect-your-computer), or project the chosen workflow needs.
4. Ask for one concrete result, review it together, and check its saved or published location before moving to the next step.
5. Use [AI replies](/designing-social-intelligence) to adjust participation, [threads](/threads) for focused work, and [Tasks](/tasks) for requested follow-ups.
