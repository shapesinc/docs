Planning together means working with different preferences, constraints, and ideas. One person wants the cheapest trip; another needs a shorter journey. One teammate wants more features; another wants to finish on time.

shapes.inc gives the group a shared place to work through those differences with a multiplayer assistant. Bring the people involved and a [Shape](/what-are-shapes) into one [chat](/ai-group-chats), share what matters, and ask it to help the group find a plan everyone can evaluate.

## Plan a trip with friends

Start with the decision you need to make, then gather the constraints: dates, budget, travel time, accessibility needs, and what each person wants from the trip.

- Ask the Shape to summarize everyone's preferences and point out where they conflict.
- Compare a small set of options against those preferences. With an appropriate [research skill](/shapeskills) available, ask for sources to check current details.
- Ask for an itinerary that explains the compromises, such as a beach afternoon and a shorter hike so both priorities fit.
- Pin the agreed plan. Use [memory](/memory) for continuity and refresh the recap as decisions change.
- [Branch off](/branch-off) a detailed side question, such as transport, and bring its conclusion back to the group.

> We want a weekend away. One person wants the coast, one wants hiking, and one cannot leave until Saturday morning. Help us compare two realistic plans. Ask about missing constraints before recommending one.

## Study together

A multiplayer assistant can help a study group decide what to cover and work through the questions that divide the group.

- Share the exam topics and ask everyone which areas they find difficult.
- Give a tutor Shape relevant material as [knowledge](/shapes101#knowledge), then ask it to propose a study plan based on the group's needs.
- Ask it to compare two explanations or walk through an example so everyone can discuss the same reasoning.
- Use [Skip turn and fast forward](/multimessages) when you want another Shape response without typing a new message.
- End with a recap of what the group covered and what still needs practice. Correct it together before using it as the next session's starting point.

Choose an [engine](/aienginecheatsheet) suited to the task. If you need outside sources or calculations, check the available [skills](/shapeskills) and verify the results against your course material.

## Build an app or a website together

Before implementation, a group needs to agree on the problem, the audience, and what to build first. Use the chat to make those choices and produce a draft everyone can review.

## What makes the group version better

- **Preferences are visible.** The assistant can respond to the needs people have actually shared, and anyone can correct its understanding.
- **Tradeoffs are explicit.** Everyone can see why an option fits some constraints better than others.
- **The work is reviewable.** A shared draft gives the group something concrete to improve.
- **Agreement stays with the people.** A recommendation helps the group decide; ask participants to confirm the plan before calling it settled.

## Set it up

1. [Create a chat](/create-a-chat) for the project and invite the people involved.
2. Add a Shape and choose a fitting [engine](/aienginecheatsheet).
3. Add the shared goal, constraints, and decision criteria to [Custom Chat Instructions](/custominstructions-chats).
4. Share reference material or configure relevant [skills](/shapeskills).
5. Tune [AI replies](/designing-social-intelligence), ask for the first comparison or draft, and review it together.
