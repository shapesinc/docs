Shapes are group assistants, built to help you all together. This page covers how to control when a shape speaks in a chat and what it contributes, using shared instructions, memory, and **AI replies**.

A shape is useful in a group when it knows what the group values, contributes its own perspective, and picks the right moment to speak.

## Three principles for a multiplayer assistant

### Earn the group's trust

Share the group's goal, constraints, and preferences in [custom chat instructions](/custominstructions-chats). Ask the shape to explain recommendations in terms of those priorities, distinguish facts from assumptions, and acknowledge what it does not know.

When it misses someone's point, correct it in the chat and ask for a revised summary. Trust grows when the group can inspect and improve the work together.

### Be an equal actor in the group

Give the shape a clear role and a perspective it can contribute. A researcher can point out missing evidence; a planning assistant can explain why an option fails a shared constraint.

Its recommendations should consider everyone in the collaboration. Ask it to explain disagreements respectfully and avoid treating one person's preference as the whole group's decision. The people involved still decide what to do.

### Proactively add value to the group

A well-timed contribution is a clarifying question, a relevant fact, a comparison, or a recap that reveals an unresolved choice. It succeeds when the group moves forward.

Use the reply triggers below to tune participation. Reply triggers only decide when a shape speaks: they do not connect calendars, email, or other apps, and they do not guarantee background work. [Skills](/shapeskills) provide built-in capabilities, and [connected apps](/connected-apps) or MCP servers can add more.

<span id="the-core-problem-presence-without-spam" />

## Balance presence and noise

An assistant that answers every message crowds out the people making the decision. One that never contributes leaves useful questions unexplored.

Start with a clear role and a small set of reply triggers. Watch whether contributions help the group, then adjust. One short question often does more than several unsolicited recommendations.

<span id="the-main-dial-free-will" />

## The main dial: AI replies

**AI replies** controls when shapes join the conversation on their own. Open the chat's **Settings** by selecting its name or avatar at the top of the chat (chat owners can also use **More actions** → **Settings**), then go to **AI settings** → **AI replies**. **AI settings** is available to chat owners and in DMs with a shape.

In chats with two or more people, **Jev (experimental)** is always on: the AI reads the conversation, joins in when it can help, and stays out of ordinary conversation. An @mention of a shape always gets a reply. See [How chats work](/chat-and-agent-modes).

Under **Auto-reply triggers**, a chat shows:

- **Jev (experimental)** uses the conversation to decide whether the AI should reply or stay quiet. You can turn it on or off in a DM with a shape; in a group it's always on.
- **Come back later**: shapes check back in if the conversation goes quiet. This is conversational follow-up, not a scheduled reminder with a guaranteed time. For reminders, ask a shape directly and check **Settings** → **Tasks**; see [Tasks](/tasks).

The other triggers (**Reply when mentioned**, **Keep the convo going**, **React to keywords**, and **Always have something to say**) appeared only in group chats set to Chat mode. Chats no longer have modes, so they no longer appear.

**Time Awareness**, under **General Settings** in the same panel, supplies time context to responses; it does not decide when a shape replies. For extra replies, see [Multiple messages](/multimessages).

## Understanding people's preferences

People often want different things from the same plan. Give the shape enough context to recognize those differences.

- **Each person's priorities** help the shape compare options fairly. Ask everyone to share the constraints and preferences that matter to them.
- **[Memory](/memory)** carries continuity across the context available to a shape. When a preference or decision matters, state it clearly and correct misunderstandings.
- **A shared recap** lets everyone check whether the assistant understood the group. Pin the current plan and revise it when agreement changes.

A shape does not recall every detail. Bring the relevant information back into the conversation when it matters.

## Coordinating multiple shapes

If a chat has multiple shapes, give each a distinct contribution: one might draft a plan while another checks it against the group's constraints.

- **Separate their roles.** Avoid several shapes repeating the same recommendation.
- **Mention the shape you want.** Say a shape's name or @mention it to direct each contribution. See [reply settings](/shapes101#activating-shapes).

<span id="voice-in-a-crowd-presets-and-personality" />

## Presets and personality in a group

A shape's [preset](/presets) and personality set how it responds. Write its guidance for a group:

- Keep replies short enough for everyone to discuss.
- Ask it to build on what people have said and ask a focused question when something is missing.
- Request the reason for a recommendation and the main tradeoff.
- Ask it to label suggestions separately from confirmed decisions.

More on response style: [Write a shape's personality and instructions](/write-a-shape).

<span id="room-level-behavior-instructions-and-guardrails" />

## Chat-level behavior: instructions and guardrails

Use chat-level controls for expectations that apply to every shape:

- **[Custom chat instructions](/custominstructions-chats):** the shared goal, decision criteria, constraints, and preferred response format.
- **[Initial messages and a welcome shape](/initialmessages):** context for people joining the chat.
- **[Moderation](/moderation):** controls such as slow mode, character limits, blocked words, and a moderator shape.

### Choose whether shapes read system messages

System messages are the notices the chat posts itself, such as someone joining or leaving, or a call ending. With **System Message Context** on, shapes read those notices along with the conversation and use them when they reply. On by default.

1. Open the chat's **Settings** and go to **AI settings**.
2. Under **General Settings**, turn **System Message Context** off. You see "System message context disabled".

With it off, shapes ignore system messages when they reply. The notices still show in the chat for people. Only chat owners see this setting. To stop join and leave notices from being posted at all, turn off **Join/Leave Messages** in [Moderation](/moderation).

<span id="test-watch-iterate" />

## Test and adjust

1. Ask the shape to summarize the group's priorities. Check whether everyone is represented accurately.
2. Ask it to compare options. Look for useful evidence, clear tradeoffs, and acknowledged uncertainty.
3. Watch when it speaks. Adjust one AI replies trigger or response-length instruction at a time.
4. If a response misses the point, correct the context or [regenerate](/regeneration) it, then review the result with the group.

<span id="the-checklist" />

## Checklist

- A clear contribution to the group's actual goal.
- Instructions that reflect everyone's stated priorities.
- Recommendations people can question and correct.
- AI replies triggers matched to the chat's needs.
- Relevant skills available and any required app or service connected before relying on outside information.
- A visible recap of confirmed decisions and unresolved questions.
