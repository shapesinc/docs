A multiplayer assistant has to work with several people's priorities at once. On Shapes, Inc., a useful Shape helps a group understand its options, surface disagreements, and make progress together.

Good behavior takes more than answering quickly. It depends on understanding what the group values, contributing a useful perspective, and choosing the right moment to speak.

## Three principles for a multiplayer assistant

### Earn the group's trust

Share the group's goal, constraints, and preferences in [Custom Chat Instructions](/custominstructions-chats). Ask the Shape to explain recommendations in terms of those priorities, distinguish facts from assumptions, and acknowledge what it does not know.

When it misses someone's point, correct it in the chat and ask for a revised summary. Trust grows when the group can inspect and improve the work together.

### Be an equal actor in the group

Give the Shape a clear role and a perspective it can contribute. A researcher can point out missing evidence; a planning assistant can explain why an option fails a shared constraint.

Its recommendations should consider everyone in the collaboration. Ask it to explain disagreements respectfully and avoid treating one person's preference as the whole group's decision. The people involved still decide what to do.

### Proactively add value to the group

A well-timed contribution might be a clarifying question, a relevant fact, a comparison, or a recap that reveals an unresolved choice. The measure of success is whether it helps the group move forward.

Use the reply triggers below to tune participation. [Skills](/shapeskills) provide built-in capabilities, and connected MCP servers can add more, but reply triggers do not themselves connect calendars, email, or other apps, or guarantee background work.

## The core problem: presence without spam

An assistant that answers every message can crowd out the people making the decision. One that never contributes can leave useful questions unexplored.

Start with a clear role and a small set of reply triggers. Watch whether contributions help the group, then adjust. A useful short question can do more than several unsolicited recommendations.

## The main dial: AI replies

**AI replies** controls when Shapes can join the conversation. Open **More actions** → **Settings** → **AI Configurations**, then find **AI replies** → **Auto-reply triggers**. The options shown can vary with your permissions and chat.

- **Reply when mentioned** lets Shapes respond when someone uses their `@name`.
- **Keep the convo going** lets a Shape continue the exchange after it has replied, without needing another mention.
- **React to keywords** lets Shapes respond to configured trigger words. Where this option is available, set keywords for individual Shapes in that chat and use **Save**.
- **Always have something to say** lets a Shape respond when nobody else replies.
- **Come back later** lets Shapes check back in later if the conversation goes quiet. Treat it as conversational follow-up rather than a scheduled reminder with a guaranteed time.

Enable triggers deliberately and adjust them when the chat becomes too noisy. **Time Awareness** supplies time context to responses; it is separate from deciding which reply triggers to enable. See [Multiple Messages](/multimessages) if you are troubleshooting extra replies or message splitting.

## Understanding people's preferences

People often want different things from the same plan. Give the Shape enough context to recognize those differences.

- **Each person's priorities** help the Shape compare options fairly. Ask everyone to share the constraints and preferences that matter to them.
- **[Memory](/memory)** helps with continuity across the context available to a Shape. When a preference or decision matters, state it clearly and correct misunderstandings.
- **A shared recap** lets everyone check whether the assistant understood the group. Pin the current plan and revise it when agreement changes.

Do not assume a Shape can recall every detail. Bring the relevant information back into the conversation when needed.

## Coordinating multiple Shapes

If a chat has multiple Shapes, give each a distinct contribution: one might draft a plan while another checks it against the group's constraints.

- **Separate their roles.** Avoid several Shapes repeating the same recommendation.
- **Use chat-specific keywords where available.** Those can help direct relevant topics to different Shapes; AI replies triggers themselves are chat settings.
- **Review [reply settings](/shapes101#activating-shapes).** Use **Reply when mentioned** when the group wants to direct each contribution, and turn off automatic triggers that cause unwanted replies.

## Voice in a crowd: presets and personality

A Shape's [preset](/presets) and personality influence how it responds. Make its guidance useful in a group:

- Keep replies short enough for everyone to discuss.
- Ask it to build on what people have said and ask a focused question when something is missing.
- Request the reason for a recommendation and the main tradeoff.
- Ask it to label suggestions separately from confirmed decisions.

For more on response style, see [When Less Is More](/shortguide), [Prompt 101](/prompt101), and the [Prompting Guides](/promptingguides).

## Chat-level behavior: instructions and guardrails

Use chat-level controls for expectations that apply to every Shape:

- **[Custom Chat Instructions](/custominstructions-chats):** the shared goal, decision criteria, constraints, and preferred response format.
- **[Initial messages and a Welcome Shape](/fandomchat):** context for people joining the chat.
- **[Moderation](/chats-101):** controls such as slow mode, character limits, and blocked words.

## Test, watch, iterate

1. Ask the Shape to summarize the group's priorities. Check whether everyone is represented accurately.
2. Ask it to compare options. Look for useful evidence, clear tradeoffs, and acknowledged uncertainty.
3. Watch when it speaks. Adjust one AI replies trigger or response-length instruction at a time.
4. If a response misses the point, correct the context or [regenerate](/regeneration) it, then review the result with the group.

## The checklist

- A clear contribution to the group's actual goal.
- Instructions that reflect everyone's stated priorities.
- Recommendations people can question and correct.
- AI replies triggers matched to the chat's needs.
- Relevant skills available and any required service connected before relying on outside information.
- A visible recap of confirmed decisions and unresolved questions.
