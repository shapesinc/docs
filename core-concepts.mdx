Six building blocks make up a chat on shapes.inc: group chats, shapes, AI Engines, memory, participation, and skills. Shapes are group assistants, built to help you all together. See [Features and limits](/features) for the current capabilities and setup guides.

The goal is an assistant that earns the group's trust, contributes as its own participant, and proactively does useful work. Configure each block toward that.

<span id="1-group-chats-are-the-room" />
<span id="1-group-chats-bring-everyone-together" />

## Group chats bring everyone together

A chat holds the people, shapes, and conversation for a shared purpose: a family trip, an apartment decision, a study project, or a team plan.

- Invite the people involved so the assistant can work with their different preferences.
- Put the goal and standing constraints in [chat instructions](/custominstructions-chats).
- Use the chat's notification, moderation, appearance, and credit settings to suit the group. Select the chat's name at the top to open its **Settings**.

See [AI group chats](/ai-group-chats) for an example and [Create a chat](/create-a-chat) for the setup steps.

<a id="two-kinds-of-chat" />

### How chats work

In every chat, a shape can search the web, run code, use connected apps, and take other actions. While a shape works, a bar above the message box shows who's working, with **Stop**. See [See what a shape did](/chat-and-agent-modes#see-what-a-shape-did).

When you're just chatting with a shape, it replies in its own voice, without actions. Your next message can go back to asking for help. There's nothing to pick or switch. See [Just chat with a shape](/chat-and-agent-modes#just-chat-with-a-shape).

Private one-to-one conversations are called **DMs**. Every DM between two people includes Tod, which stays quiet until one of you addresses it; see [DMs between people](/chat-and-agent-modes#dms-between-people). See [How chats work](/chat-and-agent-modes).

<span id="2-shapes-are-the-participants" />

## Shapes are the participants

A **shape** is a customizable AI assistant with a name, personality, instructions, and a role. One shape might research options; another might help with writing or learning.

Give the shape a role that serves the group. Ask it to explain its recommendations, notice missing perspectives, and accept corrections from the people involved. Creator permissions control who can edit the shape; they do not make the creator's opinion the group's answer.

Start with **New chat**. To use a specific shape, [choose an existing shape](/how-to-talk-to-shapes) or [make your own](/how-to-make-a-shape); customization is optional. See [What are shapes?](/what-are-shapes).

<span id="3-models-are-the-engine-underneath" />
<span id="3-ai-engines-generate-responses" />

## AI Engines generate responses

Each shape uses an **AI Engine** to generate its responses. Instructions define the role; the AI Engine affects reasoning, writing, supported inputs, and skill use. The AI Engine built by shapes.inc appears as **Formless**.

Choose an AI Engine for the task and check its current cost. Some AI Engines are free; premium AI Engines use [Shape Credits](/credits). Features and availability vary by AI Engine. To switch in a chat, use the composer's **+** menu → **Engine**. To see which AI Engine wrote a reply and what it cost, open **Generation details** from the reply's ⋯ menu.

Use the [AI Engine guide](/aienginecheatsheet) and the [live engine list](https://shapes.inc/engines). If a response uses credits unexpectedly, check the selected engine, fallback behavior, and [credit settings](/faq/why-is-a-free-engine-costing-credits).

<span id="4-memory-provides-continuity" />

## Memory provides continuity

A shape can use several kinds of memory:

- **Short-term memory (STM):** recent messages included in the current context.
- **Chat history:** the moments from the whole chat that relate to the current message, including what happened before the shape joined.
- **Long-term memory (LTM):** summaries shapes write about a conversation, recalled when relevant. In a group chat, every shape in it shares the same summaries.
- **Saved notes:** things people asked a shape to remember, for themselves, a chat, or a workspace. A standing preference you state about yourself is saved as a personal note on its own.
- **Workspace memory:** relevant background from the other chats in the same workspace you are in.

Together these keep track of a group's preferences, decisions, and recurring constraints. Memory is selective, and context windows have limits. Save a critical requirement as a note, and review remembered information when plans change.

Open **More actions** → **Manage memories** in a chat, or **Settings** → **Memories** to view, add, edit, and delete personal, chat, and workspace memories. The [memory guide](/memory) explains how. [Context windows](/contextwindows) explains how much information an engine can use at once.

<span id="5-participation-determines-when-a-shape-contributes" />

## Participation determines when a shape contributes

In group chats, shapes reply when they can help and stay out of ordinary conversation. Mention a shape with `@` or say its name to direct a contribution. Under the chat's **Settings** → **AI settings** → **AI replies**, chat owners can turn on **Come back later** so a shape returns after a quiet period.

Start with a pace that leaves space for people. In the instructions, explain what makes an interruption useful: an overlooked requirement, new information, or a concrete next step. Proactivity should be judged by the value of the contribution.

See [Shape behavior](/designing-social-intelligence) for the controls and suggested setups. See [reply settings](/shapes101#activating-shapes) if a shape is silent or replies too often.

<span id="6-skills-let-shapes-take-action" />

## Skills let shapes take action

**Shape Skills** give an assistant capabilities beyond a text response. Depending on the available skill and the engine, it can generate an image, research a page, run code, or use a connected service. Built-in skills are included automatically.

To let shapes use apps like Gmail, Google Calendar, or Notion in your chats, connect them under **Settings** → **Connected apps**; see [Connected apps](/connected-apps). Shapes can also work on a computer you link; see [Connect your computer](/connect-your-computer). External MCP servers require setup, supported authentication, and access to the outside service. Connecting one service does not give a shape access to other people's private accounts. Review the skill's permissions and what it can change before using it in a shared chat.

Follow [Shape skills](/shapeskills) for setup and troubleshooting. If an action fails, check whether the skill is available, whether the engine supports it, and whether the connection still has the required access.

## Putting it together

For a family trip, start with **New chat** and invite everyone. Share dates, budgets, and preferences. Ask the shape to compare possible plans, identify the tradeoffs, and draft an itinerary. Update the shared context as people make decisions.

The assistant contributes the research and drafts it can support. The group reviews the result and chooses what to do next.
