An AI assistant can help you prepare a plan on your own. When other people need to agree to it, the task changes. Their preferences, information, and objections belong in the process too.

A multiplayer assistant works in the group chat where that discussion takes place. On Shapes, Inc., everyone can contribute context and respond directly to the assistant's proposals.

## The Missing Information Belongs to Other People

Imagine researching a couch for your roommates. You ask for affordable options and receive a reasonable shortlist. Then one roommate mentions allergies, another checks the doorway, and a third explains why a modular design matters.

The first answer was based on an incomplete brief. A group chat makes it easier to collect those constraints before investing more work in the recommendation.

> Ask each of us what matters in a couch. Then compare our links against the combined requirements. Show the tradeoffs and any measurements or material details we still need to verify.

## Feedback Belongs Beside the Proposal

When an answer is copied out of a private conversation, the rest of the group may not see the assumptions behind it. In a group chat, people can question the same proposal, supply missing evidence, and ask for a revised version.

That does not make the AI automatically correct or neutral. It gives the group a common place to inspect its work. Ask it to represent disagreements accurately and explain what would change its recommendation.

## A Useful Assistant Earns Its Place

Our goal is an assistant that earns the group's trust, participates as its own actor, and proactively contributes useful work. It should help the group make progress without treating one person's preference as everyone's decision.

You can guide that behavior with [custom instructions](/custominstructions-chats). Define the task, explain how the group wants to make decisions, and ask the assistant to surface missing information. Its responses still depend on the engine, available context, and configuration.

## Choose the Format for the Work

A private conversation is useful for individual preparation and information you do not want to share. A group chat is useful when several people need to contribute, review, or agree.
