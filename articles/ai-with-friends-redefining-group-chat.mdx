A group conversation often contains everything needed to make progress: someone's research, another person's concern, a budget, and a date. Bringing those pieces together takes work.

Shapes, Inc. puts a multiplayer assistant in the group chat so the people involved can work through the decision together. Your Shape can help organize what has been said, compare alternatives, and draft the next step for the group to review.

## Start With the Decision

Give the group chat a concrete purpose: "Choose a weekend trip," "Plan our science project," or "Find a couch that works for all three roommates."

Ask each person to share their must-haves and flexible preferences. Include deadlines, budget, accessibility needs, and any links or other information the group should consider.

> We need to choose a weekend trip. Before recommending anything, summarize what each person cares about and ask about missing constraints.

## Compare the Compromises

There may be several reasonable answers. Ask your Shape to show why each option works for some preferences and falls short on others.

> Compare these three destinations against our budget, travel time, and activity preferences. Separate what we know from what needs checking. Suggest a compromise and explain who would need to give something up.

Everyone can correct the summary and add context. A recommendation becomes more useful when the people affected can examine its reasoning in the same chat.

## Turn Agreement Into a Next Step

Once the group agrees, ask for a short recap:

- What did we choose, and why?
- What is still undecided?
- Who will do what next, and by when?

Use [custom instructions](/custominstructions-chats) to keep the assistant focused on this workflow. Built-in [Shape Skills](/shapeskills) are included automatically, and connected MCP servers can provide access to external services. A drafted plan does not mean that a reservation, purchase, or calendar change has happened; check the result of any requested action.

## When the Conversation Gets Stuck

If the Shape keeps agreeing with the latest speaker, ask it to revisit all stated constraints. If it misses an older detail, restate the current brief and review [context windows](/contextwindows) and [memory](/memory). If an answer needs improvement, give specific feedback before trying [regeneration](/regeneration).
