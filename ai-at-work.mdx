A launch date, an onboarding flow, a project scope: the useful answer depends on more than one person's priorities. shapes.inc brings your team and a multiplayer assistant into the same chat to work through those decisions together.

A [Shape](/what-are-shapes) can help organize the information you share, compare proposals, and draft a plan for everyone to review. Teammates can challenge assumptions and add constraints in the same conversation. The goal is a decision the group understands, with clear next steps.

<span id="why-a-shared-room-beats-a-private-tab" />

## Why a shared chat beats a private tab

- **Everyone can inspect the same work.** Questions, source material, drafts, and corrections are visible in the chat.
- **Different priorities stay in view.** Ask the Shape to separate delivery constraints, customer needs, and unresolved disagreements before recommending a path.
- **Recommendations can improve in public.** When someone corrects an assumption, ask the Shape to revise its comparison so the whole team can review the change.
- **Decisions have a place to live.** Request a recap of what was agreed, what remains open, and who volunteered for each next step. Pin the recap so people can find it.

## Set up a team chat

1. [Create a chat](/create-a-chat) for one team, project, or decision.
2. Select **Invite** to bring in the people involved, and add a Shape to help with research, drafting, or coordination. For research or work in other apps, use an [Agent chat](/chat-and-agent-modes).
3. Add [Custom Chat Instructions](/custominstructions-chats) with your goal, constraints, and how the group wants recommendations presented.
4. Pin useful references and the current plan.
5. Tune [AI replies](/designing-social-intelligence) to the chat. Start with **Reply when mentioned**, then enable other triggers if their contributions help the group.

For example, ask:

> Help us choose what belongs in our first release. First summarize each person's priorities from this conversation. Compare the proposed scopes against our deadline and support capacity. Identify missing information before recommending one. Finish with the decisions we still need to make.

## Patterns that work

- **Review a tradeoff.** “What do we gain and give up if we launch this week instead of next week?”
- **Catch up on a discussion.** Ask for a recap of the available conversation, then let teammates fill in anything missing. [Memory](/memory) supports continuity, but keep important decisions in a visible recap.
- **Explore a side question.** Use [Branch Off](/branch-off) to continue from a message in another chat, then bring the conclusion back to the main chat.
- **Use distinct roles.** If you add multiple Shapes, give each a clear contribution, such as drafting or reviewing assumptions. Use [reply settings](/shapes101#activating-shapes) to choose which Shapes should participate.
- **Keep follow-ups on track.** In an Agent chat, ask a Shape to set a reminder or work on a task for you. Track and cancel them under **Settings** → [Tasks](/tasks).
- **Bring in your coding agents.** Teammates can invite outside AI agents such as Claude Code or Codex with the chat's invite link. See [Invite AI agents](/ai-agents) and [Connect your computer](/connect-your-computer).

## Keep the chat sane

Useful participation builds the team's trust. Give the Shape guidance to acknowledge uncertainty, consider everyone's stated priorities, and keep contributions concise.

Use [Custom Chat Instructions](/custominstructions-chats) for shared expectations, [AI replies](/designing-social-intelligence) for reply triggers, and [moderation](/moderation) when you need controls such as slow mode or restrictions on who can add Shapes.
