Custom Chat Instructions give shapes shared guidance for a chat. Use them to explain what the group wants to accomplish, which preferences matter, and how recommendations should be presented.

## How to Add Custom Chat Instructions

1. Open the chat you want to customize.
2. Open **More actions** (**⋯**) at the top of the chat and select **Instructions**.
3. If **Preset Templates** is shown, select **Custom instructions** to open the text box.
4. Add the group's guidance, then click or tap outside the box to save.

You can also select the chat's name at the top to open its **Settings**, then choose **AI settings** → **Instructions**. Chat owners can edit chat instructions. In a DM with a shape, you can edit them yourself.

Where **Preset Templates** is shown, choosing a built-in template saves it right away and hides its instruction text. Choose **Custom instructions** to view or edit the current instructions. Where there is no template picker, write directly in the instructions box.

Keep instructions clear and focused. Shorter instructions are usually easier for shapes to follow.

### Agree on the guidance as a group

For a shared apartment decision, each roommate can post their budget, measurements, and preferences in the chat. A chat owner can collect the agreed constraints in **Instructions**, so later replies use the same starting point. Keep questions and corrections in the conversation, and ask an owner to update the shared instructions when the group agrees on a change.

When the budget or requirements change, an owner updates the shared instructions and the group reviews the next recommendation in the chat. If saving fails, reopen the instructions and check what was saved before relying on the change. Editing instructions affects future replies; it does not rewrite earlier recommendations or confirm a decision on everyone's behalf.

## Chat instructions and your preset

Chat instructions and your personal preset work together:

| | Chat instructions | Your preset |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Where | The chat's **Settings** → **AI settings** → **Instructions** | **Settings** → **Personalization** → **Your preset** |
| Applies to | Replies in this chat, for everyone | Replies to you, in every chat |
| Who can edit | Chat owners, or you in a DM with a shape | Only you |
| Length | Up to 128,000 characters | Up to 8,000 characters |

Use chat instructions for what the group shares, such as the goal, budget, and deadline. Use your preset for how you like shapes to reply to you. In a chat's **Settings**, **Profile & credits** shows that your preset applies there too. See [Personalization](/personalization).

### Set the shared goal

Explain what the group is trying to decide or make. For example: “We are choosing a couch for our shared apartment” or “We need a plan for our first project milestone.”

### Define useful contributions

Ask shapes to organize information, compare alternatives, draft a plan, or identify an unanswered question. If several shapes are involved, explain what each should contribute.

### Keep constraints available

Include the budget, deadline, agreed requirements, and preferences people have shared. These instructions provide ongoing context for the chat; they do not guarantee permanent recall of everything discussed. See [Memory](/memory) for how conversation memory works.

### Make recommendations useful to everyone

Ask shapes to consider the whole group's stated priorities, explain tradeoffs, and separate suggestions from confirmed decisions. Update the instructions when the group's goal changes.

## How It Works

Your chat instructions are added whenever a shape responds in that chat. This helps keep its contributions aligned with the goal, context, and rules you set.

> **Note:** chat instructions do not replace a shape's personality. They add extra direction for that chat so you do not have to repeat yourself.

Instructions guide responses. They do not enable [skills](/shapeskills), grant access to outside apps, switch the chat between [Chat and Agent](/chat-and-agent-modes), or set [AI replies](/designing-social-intelligence) triggers.

## Example: a shared apartment decision

> We are choosing a couch for our shared apartment. Consider the preferences each roommate shares, including budget, dimensions, comfort, and cleaning needs. Ask when a constraint is missing. Compare a few options and explain the main tradeoffs. Distinguish information we provided from assumptions or details that need checking. Keep responses concise. Treat a choice as agreed only when we confirm it.

## Tips for Great Instructions

- State the shared goal before describing response style.
- Include the constraints that affect the decision.
- Ask for a short comparison, a focused question, or a draft the group can review.
- Revise outdated instructions when plans or preferences change.
- Ask the shape to summarize its understanding so everyone can correct it.
- Keep your [context window](/contextwindows) in mind.
