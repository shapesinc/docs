Yes. shapes.inc is a web app, and it works with screen readers through standard web accessibility features. This is what it does.

## Getting around

- **Skip to content** is the first stop when you press `Tab` on a page. It jumps past the sidebar to the main content.
- The main content is a landmark, and the message list in a chat is a region named **Messages**.
- Buttons that show only an icon have spoken names, for example **Message options**, **More actions**, and **Dictate or record voice**.
- In the chat list, the open chat is marked as current, and unread and muted chats say so after their name.
- Switches and sliders in **Settings** are labeled with the name of the setting.

## What gets announced

- **New messages:** while you are at the bottom of a chat, a new message is announced as "New message from" and the sender's name. The message text is not read out for you; move to the message to read it.
- **Typing:** who is typing is announced.
- **Notices:** confirmations are announced politely, and errors are announced right away.

## Keyboard

`Cmd+/` on a Mac or `Ctrl+/` on Windows opens the **Keyboard shortcuts** list. `Cmd+K` or `Ctrl+K` searches your chats, and `Shift+Esc` moves focus to the message box. See [Commands and shortcuts](/commands-and-shortcuts#keyboard-shortcuts).

## Hear a message in the shape's voice

Open the **⋯** menu on a shape's message and select **Read out loud**. This is separate from your screen reader and uses account credits or the daily free allowance. See [voice messages](/how-to-receive-voice-messages#3-read-out-loud).

## Limits

- The new-message announcement is in English, whatever **App language** is set to.
- Some parts of the app are less complete than others. If a control has no name or something is not announced, tell us through **Settings** → **Feedback** and say which screen it was on.
